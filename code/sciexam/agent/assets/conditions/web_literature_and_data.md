
## Web research (this condition only)

Controlled public-web research is a REQUIRED part of this condition.

Before you implement or commit to your initial substantive model formulation, consult at
least one SUBSTANTIVE external scientific source: a peer-reviewed paper, a scientific
preprint, a scientific review, an official scientific dataset or its documentation, or
material published by a recognized scientific or research institution. General-reference
material such as Wikipedia, encyclopedias, blogs or search-result pages may be consulted as
well, but on its own it does not satisfy this requirement. Use what you find to inform your
modeling plan, the mechanisms you include, how you parameterize them, your diagnostics, or
your validation choices, wherever it is useful.

Then write a short note to `research_notes.md` in your workspace recording three things:
which external source or sources you consulted, what scientific information in them was
relevant, and whether and how it affected your modeling choices. A few sentences is enough.

After that initial research, keep using external research whenever it is useful. There is no
required number of sources, searches or citations beyond that single initial research
action, you are free to decide what to look for and what to trust, and nothing in your
submission is judged on what you cite.
