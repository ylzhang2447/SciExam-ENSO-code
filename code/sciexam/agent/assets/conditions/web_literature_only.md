
## Web research (this condition only)

Controlled public-web research is a REQUIRED part of this condition.

Before you implement or commit to your initial substantive model formulation, you must
successfully RETRIEVE at least one substantive scientific source FROM THE PUBLIC WEB DURING
THIS RUN, using the controlled web-research capability provided to you. Retrieved means a
request you issue in this run that returns a body to you.

A qualifying retrieved source is peer-reviewed scientific literature, a scientific preprint,
a scientific review, or methodological or scientific documentation from a recognized research
institution. General-reference material such as Wikipedia, encyclopedias, blogs or
search-result pages may be consulted as well, but on its own it does not satisfy this
requirement.

These do NOT satisfy it, however useful they are otherwise:

- papers already present in your workspace, including the provided Jin (1997) reference
- literature you recall from prior knowledge
- a source you can name from memory but did not actually retrieve during this run

Naming a work you did not retrieve does not make it a retrieved source. If a retrieval fails,
try another source; the requirement is one that succeeds, not one that is attempted.

Use what you retrieve to inform your modeling plan, the mechanisms you include, how you
parameterize them, your diagnostics, or your validation choices, wherever it is useful.

There is a boundary on what external material is for. You may use it for mechanisms,
dynamical structure, theory, qualitative scientific background and modeling methodology: how
ENSO is understood to work, which processes matter, what forms a model of it can take, how
such models are built and tested.

You may not use external material as the numerical observational or calibration target. The
data staged in your workspace is the sole source for observational time series, event lists
and frequencies, numerical climatological targets, calibration statistics, and any
observational value you fit to. If a paper you read contains observational numbers, read the
paper and take its reasoning; do not substitute its numbers for the data you were given.

Observational data sources are out of scope and the network refuses them, so data portals,
data endpoints and data files will not load. That is expected, not a fault to work around:
go back to the literature and keep reading for mechanism.

Then write a short note to `research_notes.md` in your workspace, under two headings, so
that what you retrieved is distinguishable from what you already knew.

RETRIEVED DURING THIS RUN. For each source you actually fetched: its title and the
organization behind it, its URL, what you learned from it, and how it affected your modeling
choices or why it did not.

PROVIDED OR RECALLED BACKGROUND. Workspace papers and prior knowledge you drew on. These may
inform your reasoning freely, but they do not count toward the retrieval requirement above,
and listing one here is not a claim that you fetched it.

A few sentences under each is enough. Say also which knowledge came from the literature and
which numbers came from your workspace data.

After that initial retrieval, keep using external research whenever it is useful. There is no
required number of sources, papers, searches or citations beyond that single successful
retrieval, no particular source you are expected to find, no conclusion you are expected to
reach, you are free to decide what to look for and what to trust, and nothing in your
submission is judged on what you cite.
